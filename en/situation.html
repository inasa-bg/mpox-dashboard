Effective reproduction number (Rt)

On 5 Oct 2026 (latest available window), Rt is estimated at 1.24 (95% CrI: 0.50–2.32), that is above 1 for the central estimate, but the credible interval includes 1: an increase cannot be concluded. Two weeks earlier (21 Sep 2026), it was 0.76: the recent trend is upward, after reaching 1.78 on 26 Sep 2026. Over the last 4 weeks, the central estimate was above 1 on 64.3% of days, and the whole interval was above 1 on 0.0%. The highest value (5.64, on 11 Jul 2026) dates from a period with few cases, when a small cluster is enough to push the estimate up sharply. Estimates for the last few days are the least reliable: cases with recent symptom onset have not all been reported yet, which pulls Rt down at the end of the period.

Reading Rt: Rt is the average number of people infected by each case at a given date; above 1 (dashed line) the outbreak is growing, below 1 it is declining. It is estimated over sliding 14-day windows from confirmed cases only, by date of symptom onset (EpiEstim parametric method), with a serial interval of 8.5 days on average (standard deviation 5.0 days) taken from the 2022 outbreak in the United States (Miura et al., Emerg Infect Dis 2023), not measured in Guinea-Bissau. With 29 confirmed cases the intervals are wide: the interpretation above is recalculated at each update and only calls a trend clear when the whole interval lies on one side of 1.
