Methods and data quality

Data for W43-2026, updated 19 Oct 2026

Data sources

  • Case line list (notification and investigation form): 181 notified cases, from 29 Jan 2026 to 19 Oct 2026.
  • Contact follow-up database: 387 contacts and 2,917 follow-up visits.
  • Base maps: 117 health areas and 11 health regions.
  • Response timeline: MINSAP/INASA, SGI-Mpox.

The data are managed by INASA. This site is regenerated automatically from these sources; the data cut-off is the most recent notification date in the line list.

Definitions

  • Notified (suspected) case: any person notified under the national case definition, whatever the laboratory result.
  • Confirmed case: notified case with a positive mpox RT-PCR.
  • Not confirmed: notified case with a negative RT-PCR (“descartado”).
  • Pending: notified case whose laboratory result is not yet available or inconclusive.
  • Epidemiological week: ISO 8601 week, Monday to Sunday (W39 = week 39).
  • Reference date for curves: date of symptom onset; if missing, date of notification.
  • Contact under follow-up: contact whose planned end of follow-up (21 days after the last exposure) has not yet been reached, with no closing visit and who has not become a case.

Data protection

No personal data are published on this site: no names, no case or contact identifiers, no addresses, no occupations. Index cases are labelled with a sequence number (“Index case 1”, “Index case 2”…). Individual records are used only for the calculations, on a controlled computer or server, and only aggregated results are published. Figures based on very small numbers (one or two people in a health area) should be interpreted and shared with care.

Data quality

How to read: these indicators flag data to complete or correct in INASA’s database. Outliers are never corrected automatically: they are excluded from the calculation concerned and counted here. Because case identifiers are typed freely (spaces, hyphens, 2- or 4-digit years), contacts are linked to cases through a “region-zone-number” key extracted from the identifier, without approximate matching; a contact whose key matches no case stays unlinked.

Analysis methods

  • Curves and maps: raw counts by epidemiological week and by health area or region. Population data are not yet integrated, so no incidence rates are calculated.
  • Positivity rate: confirmed cases / cases tested with a result (pending cases excluded).
  • Effective reproduction number (Rt): EpiEstim parametric method (Cori et al., 2013) over sliding 14-day windows, from confirmed cases by date of symptom onset; serial interval of 8.5 days (standard deviation 5.0 days), from Miura et al., Emerging Infectious Diseases 2023 (2022 outbreak in the United States). This interval was not measured locally and numbers are small: estimates are indicative.
  • Symptoms: confirmed vs not confirmed compared with Fisher’s exact test, odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals; combinations computed only on cases with all the signs concerned recorded; sensitivity, specificity and predictive values computed against the RT-PCR result.
  • Active contacts: contacts for whom the current date falls between the start and the planned end of their 21-day follow-up.

Limitations

  • The last two weeks are incomplete (reporting and laboratory delays).
  • The notification form has no field yet on case outcome: deaths are not shown.
  • Hospitalisation, isolation and epidemiological link are not shown, for lack of reliable information in the forms.
  • The contact database no longer has an explicit follow-up status: status is derived from visits (“Terminado” visit = follow-up completed; “Tornou-se caso” = became a case; planned end not yet reached = under follow-up; end passed without a closing visit = “not closed”).
  • Symptom comparisons rest on 33 confirmed cases: confidence intervals are wide.

Updates

The site is regenerated whenever INASA updates the data: all results are recalculated from the database, with no manual entry. Only the background text on the home page and the timeline are edited by hand.
